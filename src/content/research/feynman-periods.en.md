## Objective

Build testable connections from Feynman-graph topology to parametric geometry and ultimately to physical observables. The goal is not merely to find closed forms for individual integrals, but to identify structures shared by graph families under geometric classification, function-space selection and physical operations.

## Central questions

- How can Symanzik polynomials, elimination structures and Picard–Fuchs equations reveal the period class?
- Are there operational graph-theoretic criteria separating MZV, elliptic, K3-type and higher-dimensional structures?
- Can parameter differentiation, cuts, UV contractions, forest subtraction and physical projection be organized into a unified calculus?
- Can geometric data make falsifiable predictions about thresholds, integral bases, RG coefficients or analytic function spaces?

## Methods and evidence standards

The main physical setting is four dimensions and the (D=4-epsilon) expansion, while retaining structures that may extend to general dimension. Every result should record its definitions, assumptions, derivation, independent checks and unresolved gaps. Numerical evidence is useful for discovery and testing, but it is not a substitute for a general proof.
